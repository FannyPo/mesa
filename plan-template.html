How it works

Three rules that make it simple

1

One meal, scaled per person

Everyone eats the same dinner. Adult 1 gets the blood-sugar portion (smaller carbs, more vegetables); the teens get bigger servings and extra carbs to match how much they burn at practice.

2

The blood-sugar rule, for Adult 1

White rice, white bread, and instant oats spike blood sugar fast. Adult 1's plate swaps in brown rice, oats, and vegetables. The teens can have the regular versions — they're working it off.

3

Batch on Sunday, fast all week

One hour Sunday means weeknight dinners are 15–20 minutes. Prep once, eat well for seven days.

Before the week starts

Sunday Prep — 75 Minutes

  • Bake 12 chicken thighs (two pans, 400°F, 40 min). Keep some whole; shred the rest → lunches and two dinners.
  • Cook a big pot of brown rice (2 lbs dry) and a pot of white rice for the teens, or just make extra brown and let teens add fruit/bread for carbs.
  • Hard-boil a dozen eggs → fast breakfasts and post-practice protein snacks.
  • Cut veggie sticks (carrots, peppers) → grab-and-go snacks for the teens.

For the teen athletes

A note on growing, active kids

Teen athletes need carbs around practice — fruit, rice, oats, and whole-grain bread before and after training help recovery. Don't put growing, active teens on Adult 1's lower-carb portions. The blood-sugar swaps are for Adult 1; the kids just need more of the real thing, timed around their sport.
